# Assignment 1. What is something you believe to be true that most people around you would disagree with?

### Rules

* No "safe" answers.
* Must be your real belief (not copied from internet).
* Minimum 50 words.

**Hint:** What do you believe about career, money, learning, discipline, relationships, health, success, life, tech industry, etc. that most people don't agree with?

## Answer

I believe that getting good grades and graduating with a degree are not enough to build a strong career in technology. Many students around me still focus mainly on completing assignments, passing exams, and getting a good CGPA. I believe practical proof of skills matters just as much, and sometimes even more. If a student graduates with good results but has never built, deployed, documented, or maintained a real project, they may still struggle when entering the industry. I would rather make mistakes while building real systems now than wait until graduation to learn how engineering actually works.

# Assignment 2. What are the top 3 objective truths you discovered through experimentation and results?

### Definition

Objective truths do not depend on opinions. They hold true regardless of how people feel.

Write each truth in this format:

**Truth:** (1 sentence)

**Evidence from my life:** (2–4 lines: what you tried + what happened)

---

## Truth #1

### Truth

I learn technical concepts faster when I apply them to a real problem instead of only reading or memorising them.

### Evidence from my life

When I only read lecture materials, I sometimes understand the theory but forget the details later. When I applied concepts through AI projects, programming assignments, GitHub work, and debugging, I understood why the concepts worked and remembered them more clearly. Building something also exposed problems that were not obvious from reading notes alone.

---

## Truth #2

### Truth

Breaking a large task into smaller and clearly defined tasks increases my chances of completing it properly.

### Evidence from my life

I have worked on projects that initially looked overwhelming because there were many components to complete. When I divided them into smaller parts such as preprocessing, model development, testing, documentation, and integration, the work became easier to manage. I also made fewer mistakes because I could focus on one problem at a time.

---

## Truth #3

### Truth

A reliable system for tracking information is more effective than depending on memory.

### Evidence from my life

I experienced this when managing different projects and also when handling orders for my small K-pop merchandise business. Manually remembering updates, order statuses, payments, and tasks became difficult as the amount of information increased. Using structured documents, spreadsheets, GitHub issues, task lists, and eventually thinking about automation made the work much easier to control.

# Assignment 4. Have you ever cut corners (unethical / dishonest / shortcut behavior — not necessarily illegal)? If yes, how did it make you feel?

### Important

* guilt
* fear
* shame
* stress
* regret
* numbness
* etc.

This is about self-awareness, not judgment.

### Answer Format

**Yes**

If Yes:

**What emotion did you feel?** (minimum 50–100 words)

## Answer

I have cut corners before, especially when I was under time pressure and focused more on finishing a task quickly than doing it properly. Afterwards, I usually felt stressed and slightly guilty because I knew the quality of my work could have been better. Even when the final result seemed acceptable, I sometimes worried that I had missed important details or did not fully understand what I had done. It made me realise that taking shortcuts may save time temporarily, but it can also reduce my confidence in my own work and create more stress later.

# Reflection – Week 1

### Biggest insight I got about myself this week

I realised that being busy does not always mean I am making meaningful progress. I often have many things to handle at the same time, including university work, projects, technical learning, own businesses and personal responsibilities. This week helped me realise that I need a clearer system to organise my priorities and turn the time I spend learning into actual skills, projects, and proof of work.

### My biggest weakness/loop I noticed

My biggest weakness is allowing tasks to build up until the deadline becomes close. When several responsibilities come at the same time, I tend to focus on whatever feels most urgent instead of working consistently on important tasks earlier. This sometimes causes unnecessary stress and makes me rush work that I could have completed more carefully.

### One system I will implement from this week (exact habit + time)

Every Sunday evening at 9:00 PM, I will spend 30 minutes reviewing the previous week and planning the next one. I will check upcoming deadlines, identify my three most important tasks, schedule my DMI sessions, and break larger tasks into smaller actions.
